Group palette

Ten hues and two neutrals for lighting groups, mixer channel accents and page panels.
Separated by CIEDE2000, spread across the hue wheel, and checked under colour-blind vision.

Adjacent pairs

Every consecutive pair, as they would appear on neighbouring strips.

Reserved — never used for identity

ΔE alone is not enough — hue separation is a separate constraint. Two colours can measure well apart and still read as one colour at two brightnesses, which is what happened to an earlier pair of cyans 19° apart. For identity labelling hue is the dominant cue, so every adjacent pair here is at least 24° apart and at least ΔE2000 18.

The blue region gets a wider gap than the reds. Human hue discrimination is poorest between roughly 185° and 290°, so that span requires 44° between members where the warm end needs 24°. Ocean and Azure sit 52° apart for that reason. It is why the palette is ten rather than twelve, and ten that are reliably distinct beats twelve with a doubtful pair.

Measured with CIEDE2000, not CIE76. CIE76 overstates differences in the blue region — an earlier version had two blues measuring ΔE 27 apart that were plainly hard to separate, because under CIEDE2000 they were only 13. Anything added to this palette must be checked the same way.

Distance from the status colours is deliberately relaxed. Identity and status appear in different places: a group colour is a 3 px strip edge, a tray tint, a panel LED, while green, amber and red live in the status bar, on mute buttons and in banners. They are not confusable in practice, so the constraint budget goes on separating the palette from itself — which is what matters when two groups sit side by side. The primary teal is the exception and keeps a wider berth, because it shares contexts with these.

Colour is a secondary signal here, not the only one. A group tray carries its name and channels carry theirs, per §24.1. That is what makes ten possible at all: demanding full separation under colour-blindness collapses the red–green axis and leaves about six usable colours. Instead, confusable pairs separate by lightness — Rose against Fern differ by 16 points of L*, Salmon against Amber by 13. Toggle the simulations: the hues collapse, the brightness difference does not.

The two neutrals sit outside the hue rules entirely, because they have no hue to separate. They are also the only members that stay reliably distinguishable under colour-blindness — everything else partially desaturates while these do not.

White reads as emphasis rather than identity. It is the brightest thing available, so a white accent dominates whatever sits beside it. Best kept for a Main or master strip rather than an ordinary group. It is deliberately a shade below --color-text-primary so that labels stay dominant over the accent carrying them.

Silver has to stay well clear of the disabled grey. #4A5B6E means not configured in the status bar and inactive on a control, so a dim grey group would read as broken. Silver sits 39 points of L* above it at ΔE2000 35.9. A mid grey is not usable at all — it lands on --color-text-secondary at ΔE 6.0 and would read as body text.

There is no true red, and that is a property of the theme. On a near-black background the only reds bright enough to see are lighter than the danger red, and a light red is a salmon; anything darker either disappears or lands on #EF4444 itself. Red is also the one status colour that fills a large control — the mute button — three pixels from a channel's accent stripe. Salmon is as close as the theme allows.

Every colour clears 6:1 on both dark surfaces, so one value carries the accent, the tray tint and the LED without a second variant. The 70° gap between Fern and Ocean is where the teal accent lives, and is correctly left to it.
